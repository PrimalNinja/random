# Trait-Based Cognitive Profiling: A Proposal

## Core problem

Current psychological classification is stuck at the surface. Labels like ADHD, autism, schizophrenia, and aphantasia are cognitive clusters — descriptions of how someone diverges from an assumed default — not definitions by mechanism. This produces two failures at once: distinct underlying configurations get lumped under one label, and single configurations get split across several. The apparent "crossover" between conditions is mostly an artefact of naming by symptom rather than by cause. The labels aren't false; they're low-resolution, and their coarseness gets installed in people — especially children — as fixed identity and mis-directed upbringing.

## Proposal

Replace labels with individual trait profiles. Describe a person as a set of measured traits rather than a category. Each trait carries a graded value, not a present/missing switch, because most cognitive traits are continuous (inner speech runs from none to constant; imagery from absent to vivid). A 4-point scale suits individual identification — e.g. *1 not present / 2 infrequent / 3 often / 4 always* — with the even number deliberately removing a fence-sitting midpoint and forcing a real call.

## Two refinements the scale needs

- **Anchor type should match the trait.** Frequency anchors (*how often*) for episodic traits; intensity anchors (*how much* — none/faint/moderate/strong) for capacity traits. Measuring a degree-trait on a frequency ruler re-creates lumping.
- **Scale shape should match the trait's real distribution.** Where a trait is genuinely homogeneous in the middle, the information lives in the tails and few points suffice. Where the population is really spread but piling onto the middle values, that trait needs finer resolution. A pile-up on the middle values can mean *real homogeneity* or *a lumping instrument* — opposite causes, opposite fixes — so scale design is per-trait, never uniform across all traits.

## Trait list size

Not small, not massive. The most common ~50 traits would likely capture a large share of meaningful variation — enough resolution to distinguish configurations that current labels fuse, without inflating into unusable complexity or false precision.

## This is stage one done properly

Mature sciences classify in two stages: sort by observable features, then let mechanism correct the surface groups (splitting surface-similar things that differ underneath, merging surface-different things that share a cause). Psychology is frozen at stage one and mistakes its scaffold for the finished structure. Trait profiling is the *right* version of stage one — it records data at the resolution stage two will need, instead of lumping it away. Real categories, if any, then emerge from where the traits actually cluster, earned from the mechanism rather than stipulated from the surface.

## Why scale matters

Sub-types only become statistically visible in a large enough corpus — millions of samples, so even rare configurations appear as clusters rather than scatter. The corpus is necessary but not sufficient: it makes the mechanistic question *askable* at the right resolution; neuroscience and causal work still supply the answer. The current bottleneck isn't absent data — every diagnosed person is already a sample — it's that the data is recorded as labels instead of traits, so even a vast population is logged too coarsely to cluster. Record the axis instead of the category, at scale, and the corpus builds itself.

## Delivery: routine school screening

The instrument should be a short, routine survey, administered from primary school onward and repeated at set intervals — modelled on the mandatory in-school health checks that were once standard (e.g. school dental checks in 1980s New Zealand). It is **screening, not diagnosis**: it flags configurations for attention, feeds the corpus, and — most importantly — identifies the child whose cognitive configuration means standard pedagogy will not reach them, *before* years of mismatch install a false account of their own ability.

Repetition matters because some traits shift with development and some do not; only re-measurement reveals which. Annual or fixed year-level administration is more realistic than the twice-yearly dental cadence.

The survey records **observations**, not mechanisms. It captures *whether* and *to what degree* a trait is present — not the person's or examiner's theory of why. Architectural or causal explanations are precisely what the corpus exists to test, and must not be baked into the instrument that gathers the data; doing so would repeat the original error of stipulating structure from above.

## Motivating case (informal)

One anchor for why this is needed: a configuration reported by an adult respondent — no inner speech, no mental imagery in any modality, no felt attention or "spotlight," no sense of mental effort, no inner narrative, and no felt sense of a distinct observing self; solutions arriving as direct knowing rather than as narrated or visualised steps; parallel branch-evaluation experienced as natural; strong affective and contextual/metadata memory; an atypical interoceptive pattern (most bodily signals consciously sensed, but heart rate not directly felt); and imagery/presence sometimes appearing only in lucid dreams.

No current label captures this cleanly. It is not autism or ADHD; it is more than aphantasia alone, because it is the *co-occurrence* of several absences plus an atypical interoceptive profile. A label system files it as "aphantasia, perhaps" and discards the rest. A trait survey records all of it. That gap — a real, describable configuration that the existing categories cannot represent — is the proposal's justification in miniature.

(The respondent's own mechanistic model of this configuration is a separate, hypothesis-level matter. Claims that one processing style is *faster* than another are testable predictions the corpus could confirm or refute — not established facts, and not part of the instrument.)

## Example traits (illustrative, not exhaustive, ~50 common)

A starting set. Each trait takes a graded value; anchor type (frequency vs intensity) and number of steps are assigned per trait.

**Inner render layers**
- Inner speech / verbal thought
- Visual imagery
- Auditory imagery ("hearing" a tune)
- Tactile / motor imagery
- Olfactory / gustatory imagery
- Felt attention / spotlight
- Sense of mental effort or exertion
- Inner narrative ("thinking in words")
- Felt sense of a distinct observing self

**Processing style**
- Solutions arrive as direct knowing vs stepwise
- Serial vs parallel branch evaluation
- Verbal vs non-verbal reasoning
- Ease of holding multiple states at once

**Interoception**
- Heart rate awareness
- Breathing awareness
- Hunger / thirst signalling
- Nausea / gut signalling
- Temperature / thermal state
- Pain signalling
- Vestibular / balance / motion
- Muscle tension / readiness

**Memory & valence**
- Affective (valence-tagging) memory strength
- Contextual / metadata memory strength
- Autobiographical / episodic recall
- Feeling-memory persistence over time
- Temporal tracking (felt vs functional)

**Perception & sensory**
- Sensory sensitivity (over/under) per modality
- Sensory-filtering / overwhelm frequency
- Synaesthetic cross-linking
- Face recognition
- Sense of direction / spatial mapping

**Attention & regulation**
- Attention sustain / drift frequency
- Task-switching ease
- Hyperfocus episodes
- Distractibility by external stimuli

**Social / mind-modelling**
- Inferring others' intent from behaviour
- Inferring others' emotional state
- Comfort with ambiguity in others' motives
- Reliance on explicit vs implicit social cues

**Language & expression**
- Producing felt opinion on demand
- Producing conventional / expected prose forms
- Literal vs figurative default reading
- Ease of narrating internal states

**Dreaming**
- Dream imagery vividness
- Lucid dream frequency
- Dream vs waking rendering difference
- Dream recall strength

## Caveat: coordination cost

Labels do one thing profiles don't yet — they *coordinate*, mobilising services, funding, and community in a single word. A profile is more accurate and harder to act on. The unfinished work isn't the measurement; it's building the profile-to-support pathways so an accurate profile routes to help as reliably as a label currently does.

## About the author

This proposal comes from Julian Cassin, and the reasoning behind it is not abstract.

In Standard 3 (1983/84, New Zealand — roughly Year 5 in Australia), I was taken out of class because I was believed to be falling behind. Testing found the opposite: not behind, but ahead of the delivered material and disengaged. The instrument had one axis — ahead or behind — and one lever — accelerate or hold. It read the direction correctly and could say nothing more, because it had no way to detect *why*. The recommendation was to advance me; my father chose to keep me with my peers. But acceleration and retention are both just positions on the same conveyor, and my mismatch was not with the conveyor's speed. It was with its format — a serial, narrated delivery aimed at minds that render their thinking. No instrument in my path was built to see that, so the option that would have mattered was never on the menu.

For most of the next forty years I assumed everyone thought the way I do. From the inside there was nothing to explain: my cognition was simply how thinking is. The anomalies I noticed were other people's — the pauses, the "let me think about it," the visible overhead. I was the reference frame, quietly cataloguing everyone else's oddness. In the late 2020s, the cultural wave of "what do you identify as" led me to reach for the nearest available category and wonder if I had Asperger's — a label that approximately matched "something is different" and was wrong about what. That is exactly what labels do: a surface-cluster you pattern-match onto because it is the shape on offer, not because it is the mechanism.

The resolving fact did not arrive until November 2025, and it was not a label. It was a group of traits: roughly 99% of people have inner monologue and/or mental imagery, and I have neither. One concrete, checkable axis did in a sentence what years of borrowed categories could not. My cognition runs headless — no inner speech, no imagery in any modality, no felt attention, no sense of mental effort, no inner narrative, no felt sense of a distinct observing self. Solutions arrive as direct knowing rather than as narrated or visualised steps. Parallel branch-evaluation is natural. Affective and contextual/metadata memory are strong. My interoceptive profile is atypical — most bodily signals are consciously sensed, but heart rate is not directly felt. Imagery and a sense of presence appear only occasionally, in lucid dreams.

I participated in a University of Queensland study on this, and am willing to do more. My own mechanistic model of this configuration is a separate, hypothesis-level matter — claims that one processing style is *faster* than another are testable predictions a corpus could confirm or refute, not established facts, and deliberately not part of the instrument proposed here.

When I found out, I was sad — and the reason is the whole argument in one feeling. It was not that something is wrong with me; nothing is. It was that for forty years I could have known how other people actually think, and did not. I could have understood the pauses, the overhead, the different protocol — and worked *with* it, even taken advantage of it professionally, instead of reading it as everyone else being slightly odd. A life could have been very different with that knowledge early. There is also a quieter sadness: I have never met anyone configured like me.

But the point of this document is that it is not only about me and them. It is about every other person who is handed too little knowledge about their own cognition too late, or handed the wrong label entirely and raised against a template that was never theirs. I got the resolving fact at fifty-something. A routine trait survey, administered from primary school onward, would have handed it to me — and to them — at nine. That is the reason for the proposal. Not a grievance about a path not taken, which cannot be proven, but a provable claim: the fact that actually explained me was a trait, available in principle the entire time, and no instrument I ever passed through was built to record it.
